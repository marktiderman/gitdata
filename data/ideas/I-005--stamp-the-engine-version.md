# Nothing in a generated artifact says which engine produced it

## Why

"Byte-identical output given identical sources is what makes drift checking possible." Sources are
not the only input — the engine is one too, and it is the one nothing records.

A consumer runs `bunx --bun @marktiderman/gitdata@0.2.0 rollup`. That version lives in **seven**
hand-typed places in one repo's `package.json` (six script pins plus a devDependency), which is why
`doctor` needs GD001 to hunt for invocations that have drifted off the declared pin. Seven copies of
one fact, maintained by hand, policed by a check — when the fact could be written down once by the
thing that knows it.

Consequences today:

- A board regenerated by a newer engine can differ from the committed one, and `rollup --check` will
  correctly call it drift while naming the wrong cause. The reader sees "your board is stale" when
  the truth is "your engine moved".
- Nothing lets a reader of a generated file answer "what produced this?" — which is a provenance
  question this project otherwise takes seriously enough to have refused `upgrade --adopt` over.

## The shape

The DERIVED-VIEW banner already at the top of every generated artifact gains the version:

```html
<!-- DERIVED VIEW — generated by gitdata 0.3.0 from data/features/*.md.
     Do not hand-edit: `gitdata rollup --check` will fail if you do. -->
```

The tension, and it is the whole reason this is an idea rather than a patch: **the banner is part of
the checked bytes.** Stamping it means every consumer's boards drift the moment the engine bumps,
turning `rollup --check` red across the estate on upgrade. That is arguably *correct* — the output
genuinely was produced by a different program — and arguably intolerable.

Three ways out, in order of preference:

1. **Stamp it, and have `--check` ignore the banner line when comparing.** Provenance for the
   reader, no drift for CI. The version is recorded without becoming a tripwire.
2. Report the engine version in `doctor` only. Cheapest; helps nobody reading a committed board.
3. Stamp it and accept the churn at release boundaries.

Option 1 is the one worth building. It requires the comparison to skip exactly one line, which is a
small, testable carve-out — and it must be a carve-out the test suite pins, or it becomes a hole
someone widens later.

## Why it fits the laws

Domain-ignorant, no query-language change, format untouched. It sits directly *on* the determinism
law, which is why option 1 exists: the goal is to record the input without making the record itself
a source of drift.

## Done when

A generated artifact names the engine that wrote it, `rollup --check` stays green across an engine
bump when nothing else changed, and a test proves the ignore applies to the banner alone and not to
any other line.
